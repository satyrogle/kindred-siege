# Depth comes from showing every decision

Shipped games did not get deep combat AI from a smarter scorer. They got it from three plain layers: a group referee that hands out turns and roles, a small strict priority list per unit, and an action layer that can only move or play a clip. The best sourced teams, big and indie, say the same two things in their own words: players credit the AI with more than it does, and a decision the player cannot see is wasted work. The first Kindred Siege study read as "basic" for two reasons the sources explain. One utility scorer per fighter is the exact design Bungie dropped because it looks random and cannot be tuned. Five clips can only show about three decisions, so anything deeper stays under the water. The fix is a stack of cheap integer layers (reaction delay, attack budget, interrupts, per fight memory, visible nerve ladder, unlockable lessons), where no layer ships without its own clip, pose or word. The sim owns timing through frame data rows, and each clip is cut to the row. Almost nothing was found on how auto battlers themselves do this, so the transfer to Kindred Siege is inference from action, tactics and fighting games, and none of it has been built or measured in the project.

Source strength tags used below: **developer confirmed** (the studio said it in a paper, talk, blog or slides that the researcher read), **second hand** (a write up of a developer talk), **commentary** (a critic, dataminer or tutorial), **fan** (wiki, modder or forum), **snippet** (only a search summary was seen, treat as a lead).

## No well regarded game shipped one scorer per unit

The owner's word "basic" matches a finding from 2005. Halo 2 runs about 50 behaviours in a tree where each child answers yes or no to "am I relevant", and the first relevant child in a priority list wins. Damian Isla says float desire scores become "almost impossible" to balance past two or three choices, and he names the symptom: murky behaviour that looks random, not intended (developer confirmed, [Handling Complexity in the Halo 2 AI](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)). The studios that do score things score **where to stand or who gets a job, never what to do**. The Last of Us multiplies 0 to 1 criteria to pick a cover post, while the "what" is a priority list of skills such as panic, advance, melee and flank (developer confirmed, [Game AI Pro 2 chapter 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)).

The shape that repeats is three layers. F.E.A.R. states the bottom one outright: its state machine has three states, go somewhere, play an animation, use an object, and the third is a kind of the second. All the thinking sits in a planner above it (developer confirmed, [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)). That bottom layer is what guarantees a decision shows, because a decision can only ever come out as a move or a clip. Killzone 3 puts a squad layer on top that never acts. It only drops orders in members' queues, and one order breaks down differently by class: the engineer places a turret, the tactician calls a drone (developer confirmed, [Game AI Pro chapter 29](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter29_Hierarchical_AI_for_Multiplayer_Bots_in_Killzone_3.pdf)). Halo 3 pours squads into a tree of prioritised tasks, each with a condition and a capacity, which Isla calls "a plinko machine" (developer confirmed, [Building a Better Battle](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)).

Indie teams got the same result with far less. **Battle Brothers** keeps utility scoring but adds three cheap moves: each behaviour scores 1 to 10, anything at 2 or under is thrown out, and one of the rest is drawn at random weighted by score. Character comes from what each type is blind to. Zombies "don't care if they're stuck in swamp, have a height disadvantage or are outnumbered". Skeletons read terrain and gear. Ambushes are withheld from undead and given to goblins (developer confirmed, [Dev Blog 27](https://battlebrothersgame.com/?p=1150)). **Into the Breach** scores plus 5 per enemy hit, plus 5 per building, minus 2 per friendly, with no lookahead and no coordination, and scores down repeating last turn's choice. Its programmer notes that players "often assume the AI is doing many things more than it actually does" (developer confirmed, attribution to Subset Games not verified, [AI write up](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1OPRNzAVQNgPtP35HzhV2ZzEqE1eCdXbWA7lEZXDdMHQ/mobilebasic)). **Hyper Light Drifter** went further. Alex Preston: "unless it's a Boss, we can't have an enemy be smart one on one", because every added enemy makes the situation much harder to read (developer confirmed, [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-hyper-light-drifter-i-balances-difficulty-with-fairness)). **Rain World** is the deepest indie case, and its depth is data: optional tracker modules per creature and a table of relationships to other creatures (developer confirmed, [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world)), with six personality numbers that surface at fixed cuts, such as Scavenger eyes boggling under 30 percent Bravery (fan, [Rain World wiki](https://rainworld.miraheze.org/wiki/Behavior)).

There is a real disagreement in the sources, and it matters here. Battle Brothers draws at random on purpose: "we always have an element of unpredictability." Guild Wars 2's utility system says always picking the top score is "highly recommended", and adds a commitment bonus so two close options do not flip (developer confirmed, [Game AI Pro 3 chapter 13](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)). Halo 2 avoids near ties altogether. For a fight that is only watched, the Halo and Guild Wars position is the safer one. Flip flopping reads as a bug when nobody is pressing buttons. A seeded draw belongs only in the places where the roll reads as intent, covered below.

## Fights stop being ping pong when a referee, interrupts and memory arrive

Ping pong is what you get when each fighter decides only when its own move ends. Shipped games break it three ways.

**A referee decides who may attack.** Kingdoms of Amalur gives the target a grid of eight slots and two integer budgets. Its worked example: grid capacity 12, soldier weight 4, troll weight 8, so a second soldier waits outside. Attack capacity 10: the troll's charge costs 6, so the soldier cannot afford his lunge (5) and settles for a swing (3). One central stage manager owns all positions. Creatures with a slot stand on an outer ring and step into the inner ring only when granted an attack, which "helps the player to determine which creatures are immediate melee threats". They give the slot back right after striking, so attackers rotate (developer confirmed, [Game AI Pro chapter 28](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter28_Beyond_the_Kung-Fu_Circle_A_Flexible_System_for_Managing_NPC_Attacks.pdf)). The Last of Us does the same with named roles as tickets. One role exists only so that somebody is always shooting, and the flanker role goes only to the best placed candidate (developer confirmed, [Game AI Pro 2 chapter 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)). Doom 2016 uses token pools per attack type, and a better placed demon can steal a token (commentary from unnamed sources, [Cyber Demons](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)). God of War reportedly lets an interrupted attacker keep its token for a beat so nobody jumps into the gap (second hand, machine made summary, [talk summary](https://videohighlight.com/v/hE5tWF-Ou2k)). The craft warning comes from a tutorial: fighters without a token must visibly do something, or the fight looks like "politely queuing" (commentary, [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/attack-token-system-ue5-group-combat-that-feels-fair)).

Flanks and pincers were mostly never coded. Amalur's waiting creatures stand in front of an empty slot, "which made for natural flanking behavior without any creature explicitly needing to know about any other creature". Jeff Orkin says F.E.A.R. had no complex squad behaviours at all. A soldier sent to the only valid cover took a back route and "it appears that the A.I. is flanking" (developer confirmed, [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)).

**Interrupts let a fighter decide during the other fighter's move.** Halo 2 has stimulus behaviours: an event such as "leader died" inserts a reaction into the tree for one or two seconds only (developer confirmed, [Halo 2 AI](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)). Elden Ring, as decompiled by the community, runs a goal stack with a weighted pick by distance band, combo strings as a queue of steps with an extra repeat on a small roll, and interrupts that roll a chance to drop everything and punish a visible action such as using an item (commentary, numbers are the author's illustrations, [The low tech AI of Elden Ring](https://nega.tv/posts/low-tech-ai-of-elden-ring.html)). Every interrupt shows by nature, because it cancels one clip into another.

**Reaction delay and a chance to fail make it look fair.** The research platform FightingICE forces a 15 frame observation delay on every bot to stand in for human reaction (developer confirmed, [Tang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.13949)). Competitive human reaction is given as about 12 to 19 frames at 60 frames per second (second hand, [The Killer Groove](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-killer-groove-the-shadow-ai-of-killer-instinct)). At Kindred Siege's 20 frames per second that is **4 to 6 ticks**. Smash Bros level 9 CPUs react in one frame, and the community lists that as a flaw in the same breath as their predictable habits (fan, [SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/AI)). Both too perfect and too repetitive get called bad AI. In an auto battler nobody is being cheated, but a reaction with no delay cannot be read by a viewer either.

**Memory inside one fight makes the second exchange differ from the first.** Killer Instinct's Shadow tracks how its actions do during a match and shifts strategy when one keeps getting punished. It also picks a lower ranked action now and then to bait (second hand, [The Killer Groove](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-killer-groove-the-shadow-ai-of-killer-instinct)). F.E.A.R.'s soldier tries the door, fails, remembers, kicks it, fails, and dives through the window (developer confirmed). Mount and Blade II fighters look at the opponent's guard direction and attack somewhere else (snippet of a developer blog, [GameBanshee](https://gamebanshee.com/3ua66)), which drew a player complaint that the AI blocks as if it has eyes in the back of its head (snippet, one user). That maps straight onto Kindred Siege's hit kinds meeting a stopping stat, with the lesson attached: limit how fast a fighter can switch its ready stop.

Academic fighting game bots go one step further with short lookahead on a forward model, and a one layer opponent model beat deeper ones (developer confirmed, [Tang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.13949)). A deterministic integer sim is a perfect forward model, so this is possible in Kindred Siege. It is the most expensive layer, its cost in a five against five fight is the researcher's unchecked estimate, and no shipped commercial game in the notes uses it. It belongs last, if at all.

## The sim owns the schedule and the clip is cut to it

On who owns timing, the sources agree. Logic decides the outcome and the schedule. Animation is authored to fit. Total War runs battle logic ahead of the display: "the logic generates the 'future' whilst the display renders the 'now'", which gives the animation layer lead time to stage what is coming (developer confirmed, [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-total-war-warhammer-ii-i-to-handle-tons-of-units-and-massive-battles)). Dead Cells is the clearest sprite case. Attacks are keyed pose to pose, in between frames go only before or after the keys, and VFX carry the motion. To nerf a weapon the team moved the keys on the timeline: "we can adjust the timing of the animations dozens of times in minutes" (developer confirmed, [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)). Even For Honor, the showcase for motion matching, kept attacks on a logic first route with hand placed events that allow interrupts (second hand, [CGWorld](https://cgworld.jp/feature/1604-gdc2016-03-2.html)). Motion matching itself needs motion capture and blending and does not transfer to sprite clips.

For Kindred Siege this means every move is a frame data row: wind up ticks, the contact tick, recovery ticks, plus named tags (cancel open, vulnerable, step). The sim schedules by the row. The clip is drawn so its contact pose lands on the contact tick. The AI reads the tags, so an enemy in tagged recovery is a punish target and an enemy in tagged wind up is a parry target. No shipped 2D sprite game was found that documents such a tag system, so this is inference from the 3D sources.

**Telegraphs need about 5 ticks.** A common enemy attack split is anticipation, attack, recovery, and attacks with randomised timing need clearer anticipation poses (commentary, [Signals and Light](https://signalsandlight.substack.com/p/how-does-enemy-attack-timing-work)). Naughty Dog made enemy tells longer in The Last of Us Part II so players could read them (second hand, [4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/364/G036471/20210722007/)). A 0.25 second reaction floor is 5 ticks at 20 frames per second. A viewer watching ten fighters needs more lead than a player locked on one enemy, so the held wind up pose is where the frames should go.

**Hit stop stays in presentation.** Street Fighter 2 froze both fighters for about 10 frames, and hits without it feel weak and are harder to see (commentary, [Critpoints](https://critpoints.net/2017/05/17/hitstophitfreezehitlaghitpausehitshit/)). Smash scales the freeze with damage, 8 to 15 frames for a mid hit across three games, capped at 30 (fan, [SmashWiki Hitlag](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)). In ticks that is roughly 1 for a light hit, 3 normal, 5 heavy, 10 for a rare big moment. The freeze must sit on the screen side or be a scheduled part of the sim for everyone, or event order shifts.

**Paired clips make a crowd read as duels.** Dawn of War 4's animation director describes "synced combat": "Every action that you see in the game really has a counterpart", with action sets keyed by matchup and size class (second hand, [FRVR citing IGN](https://frvr.com/blog/news/warhammer-40000-dawn-of-war-4-massively-expands-the-originals-sync-kill-system/)). Total War authors matched exchanges but keeps the special ones rare: "rare enough to make them feel special and epic when they do happen" (developer confirmed, [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-i-total-war-warhammer-ii-i-to-handle-tons-of-units-and-massive-battles)). Sprites cannot blend, so the sprite version is a small matrix: each hit kind against a handful of responses (block, parry, dodge, hit, heavy hit), sharing one contact tick at a fixed pixel spacing. Because the sim knows the roll before contact, the screen can start the right response clip early.

**State must be announced.** Bungie's first Halo AI goal was that the player understands what the AI is doing, through three channels: speech, posture, and one time gestures (second hand, [summary of the 2002 talk](https://henryfinalproject.blogspot.com/2019/04/the-illusion-of-intelligence-talk-by.html)). F.E.A.R. is blunter: "There is no point in spending time and effort implementing squad behaviors if in the end the coordination of the A.I. is not apparent to the player", and "Vocalizing intentions can sometimes even be enough, without any actual implementation." Its soldiers also explain inaction: a pinned man says "I've got nowhere to go!" so he looks stuck, not stupid (developer confirmed, [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)). Slay the Spire found hidden random enemy moves read as "too random" and went through about ten versions of its intent display (second hand, machine summary, [talk notes](https://readray.ai/notes/76667)). The display of the decision cost more work than the decision.

For games that are watched, the few sources point one way. Vanillaware put extra effort into animation and "intentionally made the characters larger" in Unicorn Overlord because the battles run on their own (developer confirmed, [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/unicorn-overlord-interview-vanillaware)). Riot's League clarity rule ties visual strength to impact (snippet, [Riot](https://na.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)). A Darkest Dungeon player mod removed the zoom from ordinary hits and kept it for crits (fan, [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/262060/discussions/4/618453594749180956/)). Quiet basics, loud turning points.

## Learning, nerve and personality shipped as tables, ladders and spoken lines

The locked features (tribes that learn from real fights, nerve that breaks) have close shipped models, and all of them are integer table work.

**Learning is recorded cases or unlocked branches, not open learning.** Killer Instinct parses each match into 400 to 700 named patterns, stores state relative to the opponent so one case covers many situations, and replays the nearest match over more than 40 metrics. Three matches give base competence (second hand, [The Killer Groove](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-killer-groove-the-shadow-ai-of-killer-instinct)). Alien: Isolation's behaviour tree has locked branches that open when conditions are met, so the alien seems to learn. Per Andy Bray, unlocks never come from player deaths, because that would be an advantage the player cannot understand (second hand with developer input, [The Perfect Organism](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)). Forza's Drivatar learned players' mistakes too, and designers added a control layer to damp the ugly ones (second hand, [How Forza's Drivatar Actually Works](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-forza-s-drivatar-actually-works)). The Alien pattern fits a rule of one lesson per fight best: a fixed list of pre built counters, each unlocked by something the player did that worked. It is deterministic by construction.

**Memory only counts when it is said out loud.** Monolith's design director says Nemesis events surface through dialogue written to fit emergent situations, and that some features need only an audio line (developer confirmed, paraphrased, [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/upgrading-the-nemesis-system-for-i-middle-earth-shadow-of-war-i-)). Press reports that only about 6 percent of orcs get the trait that shames the player, and that rare loud traits are what players remember (snippet, [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-guy-who-designed-shadow-of-wars-orcs-is-super-proud-that-theyre-such-dicks)). The system is patented by Warner Bros (commentary, [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/middle-earth-shadow-of-mordors-nemesis-system-was-patented-by-warner-bros)), and the patent's scope was not checked.

**Nerve reads best as a short named ladder.** Battle Brothers changed morale from a hidden number to five visible states: Confident, Steady, Wavering, Breaking, Fleeing. Checks fire on named events (an ally cut down, a heavy wound, an enemy slain), move one step, and weigh who fell and how close. A rallied fighter returns to Wavering, not Steady. The stated reason is that the player sees who is about to break and why (developer confirmed, [Dev Blog 20](https://battlebrothersgame.com/?p=883)). Halo 3 has the squad version: "Leader death breaks followers" (developer confirmed, [Building a Better Battle](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)). Darkest Dungeon splits the break by character: the same full meter gives one of seven named afflictions or, rarely, one of five virtues, staged with a banner and a pause, and each self willed act is led by a class bark and an icon (developer confirmed, [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-darkest-dungeon-s-i-affliction-system)). Dwarf Fortress shows the cost of the opposite choice: stress is hidden until it bites, so players need text to follow it (fan, [Dwarf Fortress wiki](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/Stress)).

**Personality is what a fighter ignores, plus one extra want.** Halo 2 styles are allow or deny lists of behaviours plus skews, so a cowardly style flees sooner (developer confirmed). The Sims 3 gives each Sim up to five traits from about 80, and a trait adds a motive (developer confirmed for the trait counts, [Evans](https://philarchive.org/archive/EVARPT); second hand for the motive mechanism, [AI and Games transcript](https://amara.org/subtitles/cVoJS4OdmVql/en/1/download/The%20Genius%20AI%20Behind%20The%20Sims.en.txt)). Cliff Harris rebuilt his space battle orders as a visible priority list of filters plus exactly one tie breaker, such as Vulture, Retaliate or Breakthrough, and rejected conditional logic because it would feel "like work" (developer confirmed, [Cliff's blog](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2025/08/15/designing-the-orders-system-for-ridiculous-space-battles/)). Tynan Sylvester's apophenia point caps all of this: players "extract more story meaning from a game event than is really represented in the mechanics", and the cheap triggers are names and a small cast (second hand, [Stanford Game Design](https://gdt.stanford.edu/how-do-you-create-apophenia/)). Five named fighters is already the right size.

## What Kindred Siege should take: eight layers, each paired with a clip

Everything in this section is inference from the sources above. Nothing here has been built or measured in Kindred Siege. The ordering by cost is the researchers' judgement.

The rule that ties it together is a contract: **no decision type without a clip, pose or word, and no reaction to anything that has not been on screen for the fighter's reaction time.** Decide first, announce second, act third. The sim commits the decision a few ticks early and logs it, and the screen spends those ticks on the tell. Words and moves should come from the same decision record. F.E.A.R. hooked its dialogue up separately and got wrong lines, such as asking the status of a man who had just died.

| Layer, cheapest first | What it decides | Taken from | What shows on screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Frame data rows with tags | When contact, cancel and vulnerable ticks fall | Dead Cells, Total War (developer confirmed) | A distinct wind up pose per hit kind held about 5 ticks, a recovery, hit stop on the screen side |
| 2. Reaction delay and follow through as stats | How many ticks of an enemy wind up a fighter must see before answering, and whether he commits | FightingICE (developer confirmed), SmashWiki (fan) | A veteran answers at 4 ticks, a recruit at 6 or 7 and sometimes freezes: a glance, then the answer |
| 3. Squad referee with weighted budget | Who may strike, how many big hits are in flight, who gets the cash on a mark | Amalur, The Last of Us (developer confirmed) | Step in to strike, step back out. Refused fighters circle, feint, guard or taunt |
| 4. Strict priority list per class, with blind spots | What to do. Score only who and where | Halo 2, Killzone 3, Battle Brothers (developer confirmed) | Each class visibly ignores something. A choice holds until its clip reaches a cancel tag |
| 5. Stimulus interrupts | A short window after "ally fell", "leader parried", "mark cashed" in which special reactions are legal | Halo 2 (developer confirmed), Elden Ring (commentary) | One clip cancels into another: a cut string, a sidestep, a punish |
| 6. Per fight memory | Stop using what got punished. Count the enemy's habit. Hold a grudge | Killer Instinct (second hand), F.E.A.R. (developer confirmed), Rain World (fan) | A changed target or hit kind, an early parry on the third repeat, a turn toward whoever hurt him |
| 7. Nerve ladder with personal breaks | One step per named event, a rally that does not fully restore, a break that branches by fighter | Battle Brothers, Darkest Dungeon, Halo 3 (developer confirmed) | A stance per nerve state, a staged break moment, a different act per branch (flee, freeze, rage, shield a friend) |
| 8. Tribe lessons as unlocked branches | Which pre built counter a chief opens after a fight, keyed to what the player did that worked | Alien: Isolation (second hand), Nemesis (developer confirmed on surfacing) | One named tell before the next fight: a line, a pose, a changed formation |

**Layer 3 does the most for the least.** It turns a five against five scrum into readable exchanges, it is a few integers in ordered state, and it fits rules the project already has: one parryable big hit at a time for the leader, and marks laid by one class and cashed by another. Granting the cash role to the single best candidate, as Naughty Dog grants the flanker, stops two fighters cashing the same mark. A fighter calling for the mark just before an ally lays it, when the sim had already scheduled it, costs one word and reads as teamwork. That is the F.E.A.R. trick used honestly. The one extra risk a deterministic sim adds is order dependence: every referee decision needs a fixed tie break and an ordered request list.

**The opposite risk is real too.** A budget that is too tight makes four of five fighters look like extras. That is the complaint about kung fu circle games, though the evidence found for it is one forum thread. The published answers are the weighted budget (several light actions plus one heavy) and waiting behaviours that are themselves animated.

**What this means for the clip list.** The first study's five clips (stand, walk, combo, flinch, die) can show roughly three decisions: move, attack, get hit. The layers above need, per fighter, about these additions: a distinct wind up and a recovery per hit kind, the paired responses (block, parry, dodge, heavy hit), a step in and step out, one or two waiting moves, a stance per nerve state, and a few one shot gestures (glance, call out, flinch at an ally's fall, break). No source gives a clip count per enemy, so this list is a synthesis, not a quoted number. Dead Cells' lesson keeps it affordable: few frames per move, held key poses, VFX for motion. A forum post makes the same point from the other side: particles and flashes are a "shortcut for avoiding animating a ton of 'anticipation' frames" (snippet, single developer, [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/11673609)). The state change is the thing to animate, not the state.

**Three things to leave out.** Motion matching and procedural pair blending need 3D rigs. Deep reinforcement learning needs float networks and gives behaviour nobody can tune by hand. Full planners earned their place where units chain steps around obstacles, which a fight with a handful of moves per class does not need. What transfers from planners is the idea of one squad order breaking down differently per class.

**A steelman of the first study.** Battle Brothers and Guild Wars 2 both shipped utility scoring and are well regarded. So the scorer was not wrong in itself. It was alone: no referee above it, no interrupts beside it, no memory behind it, and too few clips in front of it. Rain World's developers add a warning for the other direction. An AI built to look after itself "might actually be super difficult and frustrating", and they tuned the world, not the brains.

## What was not found

**Auto battlers.** No developer source was found for how Teamfight Tactics, Dota Underlords, Mechabellum, Super Auto Pets, Backpack Battles or The Bazaar build unit AI or present autonomous decisions. Mechabellum's targeting rules come from a wiki of unknown status. Riot notes on slowing TFT animations for readability could not be confirmed. Only a League clarity post and a League pacing comment turned up, both as snippets. This is the biggest hole, because it is the closest genre.

**Sprites and AI together.** No source ties AI decisions to sprite animation budgets or frame counts. No per enemy clip counts were found for Hollow Knight, Dead Cells, Owlboy or Darkest Dungeon. No shipped 2D sprite game was found that documents animation tags feeding its AI, or root motion for sprites. Every link between the layers and the clip list in this report is inference.

**Watching AI fight AI.** No controlled study of what viewers find readable was found, apart from one paper on tuning a search bot toward highlight moments, which was not opened.

**Fighting game CPUs.** No developer source was found for Street Fighter, Tekken, Mortal Kombat or Guilty Gear CPU logic, for Tekken's Ghost, or for Virtua Fighter 4's trained AI. The Killer Instinct detail comes from a write up of the talks, not the talks.

**Action games.** FromSoftware has published nothing found here. The Elden Ring material is community decompiles with illustrative numbers. No developer source was found for Batman Arkham, Ghost of Tsushima, Sifu, Absolver or For Honor's shipped bots. Sifu attack tokens are unconfirmed. Doom's token details rest on a commentator's unnamed sources. The God of War details rest on a machine made summary, and two summaries disagree on whether tokens are fixed or dynamic.

**Talks not read.** The Nemesis talks are video only, so the data kept per orc and the trait rules are not developer confirmed. Popular claims that orcs adapt to spammed moves or cheat death were not verified. The full 2002 Halo talk (27 pages), the Darkest Dungeon II presentation talk from 2024, the Dawn of War 4 articles on how its combat director picks a pair, and the Battle Brothers follow up AI post were not reached. The 18 frame telegraph rule and the 2 to 20 frame hit stop range each rest on one unconfirmed source.

**Not covered at all.** Rival systems in roguelikes, per fighter personality in a shipped squad auto battler, and failure modes of deterministic lockstep AI.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. The first study asked how smart one fighter's choice should be. The sources say the unit of design is the pair: a decision and its tell, a striker and a responder, a mark laid and a mark cashed, a lesson learned and a line that says so. Depth under the water is cheap. It is integer tables, blind spots, counters and a referee, all of which a deterministic sim handles easily. The cost sits above the water, in clips, held poses and words. That makes art budget the real limit on AI depth, not code.

It also suggests an order of work the sources support but nobody has tested in this genre. Build the referee and the tells first, because they change what a viewer sees in every exchange. Add memory and nerve branches next, because each one produces a visibly different act. Leave lookahead search until a watched fight shows a gap that the cheaper layers cannot fill.
