# AI memory, personality, emotion, morale and learning across encounters

Scope note: research for Kindred Siege (deterministic integer auto battler, rival tribes learn from the player's real fights, fighters have nerve that breaks). "Confirmed" below means a developer said it in a talk, slide deck, dev blog, book chapter or attributed interview. Wiki and fan numbers are flagged as such. Every design suggestion for Kindred Siege sits under Inferences, never under Cited Findings.

## 1. The Nemesis system (Shadow of Mordor, Shadow of War): memory, traits, promotions, and how it surfaced

### Takeaway
The Nemesis system is a persistent roster of procedurally made named enemies that record what the player did to them and play it back as voice lines, traits and rank changes. The developer sourced detail I could reach is thin on exact rules (the full GDC talks are video only), so the confirmed points are about design intent and a few mechanisms, and the trait level rules come from press and wikis.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed (Monolith, GDC 2018 abstract): Chris Hoge's talk "Helping Players Hate (or Love) Their Nemesis" covers the design philosophy of both games, and names the goal as long term relationships between the player and procedurally created orcs, maximising both positive and negative player emotion. Source: [GDC Vault listing](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025150/Helping-Players-Hate-(or-Love)); summary at [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-helping-players-hate-or-love-their-nemesis)
- Confirmed (Bob Roberts, design director, paraphrased by Game Developer): in Shadow of War the team moved away from relationships that need a death. An ally can betray the player and become the nemesis. Roberts calls betrayal a "knob" the team turns when the player is doing too well. Source: [Game Developer, Upgrading the Nemesis System](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/upgrading-the-nemesis-system-for-i-middle-earth-shadow-of-war-i-)
- Confirmed (Roberts, same article): system driven events are surfaced through dialogue lines written to fit emergent situations. Some features need only an audio line, others need several departments. Hand authored story orcs are released into the open Nemesis roster after their story role ends. Source: [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/upgrading-the-nemesis-system-for-i-middle-earth-shadow-of-war-i-)
- Confirmed (Roberts, same article): the team learned to treat unscripted outcomes of the simulation (an orc general killing a warchief nobody placed) as features. Source: [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/upgrading-the-nemesis-system-for-i-middle-earth-shadow-of-war-i-)
- Press report of Hoge's talk (not checked against the recording): only about 6% of orcs get the "Humiliator" perk that shames the player after each win, and rare traits are what make single orcs stick in players' memory. Source: [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-guy-who-designed-shadow-of-wars-orcs-is-super-proud-that-theyre-such-dicks) (page body could not be fetched, this is from the search summary)
- Press (Tom's Hardware, on Shadow of War): the system tracks several data points from past fights and gives the orc a specific voice line that calls out the earlier event. Source: [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/nemesis-system-shadow-of-war%2C35988.html)
- Press and guides: an orc's progress is not erased when the player dies. The roster updates when the player is killed or leaves an area. Ways to build a nemesis include fighting them, making them retreat, dying to them, sending death threats. Source: [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/shadow-mordor-nemesis-system-amazing-how-works); [Game Revolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/features/341153-guide-nemesis-forge-system-shadow-mordor)
- Guides (Shadow of War): each captain has a set of traits that set his strengths and weaknesses in combat, and can gain new traits through battle or training orders. The player gets this intel by interrogating "worms". Captains can be blood brothers, and killing one makes the other seek revenge. Source: [Game Revolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/guides/350961-shadow-war-nemesis-system-changes-shadow-mordor); [Shadow of War wiki](https://shadowofwar.fandom.com/wiki/Nemesis)
- Critique: late in Shadow of Mordor the game needed less and less interaction with the captains, so the system faded. Source: [Inverse](https://inverse.com/article/33370-middle-earth-shadow-of-war-needs-to-fix-the-nemesis-system)
- The system is patented by Warner Bros. Source: [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/middle-earth-shadow-of-mordors-nemesis-system-was-patented-by-warner-bros)

### Inferences
- The mechanism is a small record per named enemy (a list of event flags and counters) plus a line of dialogue or a visible mark per flag. Nothing in it needs floats or randomness at read time, so it fits a deterministic integer sim: the record is state, the surfacing is presentation.
- The memory only matters because it is said out loud. Monolith's own framing is that one audio line can be a whole feature. For Kindred Siege the match is: every lesson a chief learns gets one named, visible tell before the fight (a line, a pose, a changed stance), so the player knows what was learned and from which fight.
- Rare traits (the reported 6% Humiliator) do more for memorability than common ones. A chief trait that shows up seldom and is loud is worth more than ten quiet stat traits.
- Betrayal as a "knob" is a director lever inside the rival system: the designers pick when it fires based on how well the player is doing. This is pacing control, not simulation.
- Strengths and weaknesses that the player can scout before a fight turn memory into a plan. This lines up with the existing Kindred Siege lock that a chief learns one lesson per fight: show the lesson list the same way.
- The critique says a rival system dies when the main loop stops forcing contact with rivals. In a roguelike the road should keep routing the player back to the same chief.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full Hoge talk (GDC 2018) or Michael de Plater's earlier Nemesis talk. They are video only. The exact data kept per orc, the trait table and the promotion rules are not confirmed from a developer source here.
- Widely repeated claims that Shadow of War captains "adapt" and become immune to a move the player spams, that orcs "cheat death" and return with scars, and that fears and hatreds trigger on set conditions: I found no developer source or solid wiki page in this pass. Treat as unverified until checked against the talk or the wiki trait pages.
- The 6% figure comes from a press write up I could not open in full.
- Patent scope and expiry were not checked. Anyone copying the structure closely should read the patent first.

## 2. Learning from the player: Black and White, Drivatars, Killer Instinct Shadows, Alien: Isolation, the Left 4 Dead Director, roguelike rivals

### Takeaway
Shipped "learning" AI is almost never open ended learning. It is one of three cheap things: record the player's actions as cases and replay the nearest match (Killer Instinct, Drivatar), unlock pre built behaviours when conditions are met (Alien: Isolation), or steer the pace from a crude intensity number (Left 4 Dead). All three are table and counter work that a deterministic integer sim can do.

### Cited Findings

Killer Instinct Shadow AI (case based reasoning)
- Confirmed via developer talks summarised by AI and Games (Bruce Hayles and Derek Neal, Iron Galaxy): the system records every action in every match and uses case based reasoning to rebuild the player as an opponent. Low level inputs are parsed into higher level patterns (setups, zoning, traps, combos, breakers, counters, taunts), about 400 to 700 patterns per match. Source: [Game Developer, The Killer Groove](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-killer-groove-the-shadow-ai-of-killer-instinct)
- Retrieval is nearest neighbour over more than 40 metrics (grounded or airborne, defensive or committing, character specific state). Distance and health are stored relative to the opponent, not absolute, so a case applies in more situations. The preceding action sequence is part of the match. Source: [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-killer-groove-the-shadow-ai-of-killer-instinct)
- Retrieved actions are ranked by perceived value, and the shadow sometimes picks a lower ranked one to bait. If an action keeps getting punished in a match it shifts to other strategies. Source: [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-killer-groove-the-shadow-ai-of-killer-instinct)
- Three dojo matches give base competence. Up to 40 matches are kept per opponent (up to about 28,000 patterns per matchup). Hayles said the 40 match cap can be too few for a full behaviour set. The metrics need a designer with deep competitive knowledge. Source: [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-killer-groove-the-shadow-ai-of-killer-instinct); talk listing at [GDC Vault, Designing AI for Competitive Games](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022992/Designing-AI-for-Competitive)

Forza Drivatar
- The first Drivatar was built at Microsoft Research Cambridge and used a Bayesian neural network to learn lines. It does not replay laps. It answers "how would you drive this car in this situation". Source: [Microsoft archive blog](https://learn.microsoft.com/es-es/archive/blogs/research/forza-motorsport-game-engine-drivatar-ai); [Game Developer, How Forza's Drivatar Actually Works](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-forza-s-drivatar-actually-works)
- The track is cut into segments and turns are classified into 12 types (hairpin, sweeper, chicane and so on). Road Atlanta's 12 turns become 27 segments. For each turn type the model learns the speed and steering the player uses and the range they stay within. Unfamiliar tracks are filled in from the player's average on similar turn types. Source: [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-forza-s-drivatar-actually-works)
- Older data is trusted less. The model learns mistakes and aggression too, then designers put a control layer on top that damps the ugly ones (ramming, doughnuts, corner cutting). Using Rewind throws away that segment's training (author's analysis, not attributed to Turn 10). Source: [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-forza-s-drivatar-actually-works)
- From Forza Motorsport 5 the Drivatar lives in the cloud as a small set of weights that other players download. Turn 10's Dan Greenawalt has said the modern version uses a form of deep learning, but the inner workings are not public. Source: [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-forza-s-drivatar-actually-works)

Alien: Isolation (two brains, unlocked behaviours)
- Confirmed (Creative Assembly, via AI and Games' write up of their material, quoting Andy Bray): two systems. A director (macro) always knows where the player is and only gives the alien a rough area to go to. The alien (micro) has to find the player with its own senses. Source: [Game Developer, The Perfect Organism](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)
- The director keeps a "menace gauge". It rises when the alien is a short walk away, when the player can see it, and when it is close on the motion tracker and can reach the player fast. At peak the director sends the alien away to other areas or into the vents, giving the player a break. If menace stays at peak, the alien goes "backstage" into vents, favouring vents ahead of where the player is likely to go. Source: [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)
- The alien's behaviour tree has over 100 nodes with about 30 top level nodes. Some branches are locked at the start and unlock as conditions are met, so the alien seems to learn. Per Bray, unlocks never come from player deaths, because that would be an advantage the player cannot understand. The shipped game also unlocks behaviours at set campaign points. Source: [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)
- The alien searches points in a deliberately suboptimal order, which causes backtracking that reads as doubt. Per Bray it teleports only twice in the campaign, both for cutscenes. He calls the target feel "psychopathic serendipity". Source: [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)

Left 4 Dead AI Director (primary source: Michael Booth's slides, read directly)
- Confirmed: the goal is "dramatic game pacing", modelled on Counter-Strike's spiky rhythm. Constant combat is fatiguing, long quiet is boring. Source: [Booth, The AI Systems of Left 4 Dead (Valve PDF)](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Confirmed algorithm: estimate each survivor's "emotional intensity" as one value, track the max of the four, and if it is too high remove major threats for a while. Intensity goes up when injured (in proportion to damage), when incapacitated, when pulled or pushed off a ledge, and when a nearby infected dies (inversely to distance). It decays toward zero over time, but does not decay while infected are actively engaging that survivor. Source: [Booth slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Confirmed phases: Build Up (full threat population until intensity crosses the peak threshold), Sustain Peak (keep full threats 3 to 5 seconds after the peak), Peak Fade (minimal threats, wait until intensity decays out of peak range so the current fight can finish), Relax (minimal threats for 30 to 45 seconds or until the team has travelled far enough, then Build Up again). Source: [Booth slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Confirmed: boss encounters are not affected by adaptive pacing. The algorithm changes pacing (frequency), not difficulty (amplitude). Booth notes the intensity estimate is crude and the pacing still works. Source: [Booth slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)

Black and White
- Richard Evans built the creature AI by combining several techniques, including perceptron training and decision tree learning. The creature had to learn from a single piece of player feedback (a slap or a stroke). Source: [Eurogamer retrospective, mirrored](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://www.eurogamer.net/black-and-white-lionhead-peter-moylneux-retrospective-google-deepmind-gemini-ai); [Lionhead postmortem, Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-lionhead-studios-i-black-white-i-)
- Forum opinion from 2001 (not a verified finding): the learning had trouble assigning reward to the right action. Source: [Slashdot thread](https://developers.slashdot.org/story/01/04/20/1321203/talking-bout-game-ais)

### Inferences
- The best fit for "a chief learns one lesson per fight" is the Alien: Isolation pattern, not machine learning: a fixed list of pre built counters, each locked, each with a plain unlock condition taken from the fight record (for example, the player won with a back row burst, so the chief unlocks "guard the back row"). It is deterministic by construction, because the unlock is a pure function of the logged fight.
- Bray's rule transfers directly: do not unlock on the player's failures. Unlock on what the player did that worked. Then the lesson always answers something the player remembers doing, which makes it readable and fair.
- Killer Instinct's two tricks are both integer friendly: store features relative to the opponent (band the values into small integers) and match on nearest case with a fixed tie break order. A tribe that "fights like the player" could store the player's squad shape and order choices as banded integer features and pick its doctrine by nearest match. No floats, no hash iteration, ordered lists only.
- Drivatar's lessons for a rival that copies the player: weight recent fights more, keep the player's quirks (they are the recognisable part), and put a designer layer on top that damps behaviour that would look broken.
- The Left 4 Dead director is four states and one counter with decay. An integer version is trivial: intensity as an int, fixed add per event, fixed subtract per tick when nobody is engaging. For a roguelike road, the same machine can pace across fights (hard fight, then a calm node), which keeps fight outcomes untouched.
- Booth's point that bosses skip the pacing logic matches a chief fight: the chief is the set piece, the director paces what comes before and after.
- A director that changes spawns mid fight would be a balance input. In a sim where the checksum must match, the director's state has to live in the snapshot like any other state, and its inputs must be sim events, not frame time.
- Black and White's one shot feedback is the same shape as "one lesson per fight": one strong signal, one stored change. The reported weakness (wrong credit) is the risk to guard against: the lesson must name the right cause.

### Gaps
- Rival systems in roguelikes specifically (for example recurring rivals in Hades style or auto battler runs): I found no developer source in this pass. Not covered.
- Which exact alien behaviours are locked and what unlocks each: the article does not list them. Popular claims (it learns to distrust the flamethrower, lockers and noise makers) were not confirmed here.
- Black and White's belief, desire, intention structure and Evans' own paper were not reached. The details above are from a retrospective and the postmortem.
- I did not find the Game AI Pro chapter on Killer Instinct. The details come from AI and Games' article on the developers' talks.
- Left 4 Dead: the slides give no number for the peak threshold or the decay rate. Figures on fan sites (such as a 0.75 threshold) are unverified.

## 3. Morale, nerve and emotion: Total War, Battle Brothers, Darkest Dungeon, XCOM, RimWorld, The Sims, Dwarf Fortress

### Takeaway
The systems that read well use a short ladder of named states (four or five steps), move one step at a time on named events, and give each state its own visible behaviour. The systems with deep hidden numbers (Dwarf Fortress) produce great stories but are hard to read without text.

### Cited Findings

Battle Brothers (official dev blog)
- Confirmed: five morale states. Confident (better hit chance and defence), Steady (no effect), Wavering (slightly worse, and a penalty on later checks), Breaking (much worse, more likely to flee), Fleeing (cannot be ordered, runs from all visible enemies). Everyone starts Steady. Source: [Battle Brothers Dev Blog 20: Bravery and Morale](https://battlebrothersgame.com/?p=883)
- Confirmed: a morale check is a roll against the Bravery stat with difficulty set by the event. Negative checks come from seeing an ally die and from heavy wounds, and failing drops one state. Positive checks come from an enemy being killed, and passing raises one state. A stronger or closer ally dying makes the check harder. Killing a strong enemy such as a chieftain lifts morale more than a minor one. Source: [Dev Blog 20](https://battlebrothersgame.com/?p=883)
- Confirmed: a fleeing fighter far enough from the nearest enemy can try to rally each turn. Success returns him to Wavering, not Steady. Undead are immune. Source: [Dev Blog 20](https://battlebrothersgame.com/?p=883)
- Confirmed design reasons: a clear five step scale so the player sees who is about to break and why; events with clear weight cause a visible response; both sides can use it (kill leaders, flank the shaky, enemies that fight with fear); veterans hold the line as losses mount. Source: [Dev Blog 20](https://battlebrothersgame.com/?p=883)
- Fan compiled, may be out of date: base chance is resolve, with penalties per adjacent enemy and a rally check that gets easier the longer the unit has fled. Source: [community spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1ZMGUIT9AaBzLIoXTY8LgMJzHQR_JuOzdluymQb4Fvi4)

Total War
- Official manual (Rome II): the general's presence raises nearby morale, elite units start higher, routing units can rally inside the general's radius, shattered units never rally, and any unit that routs after the general dies is shattered at once. Source: [Total War: Rome II manual, Morale](https://r2enc.totalwar.com/en/manual/single-player/0087_enc_page_battle_play_phase_conflict_morale)
- Analysis and guides: broken units flee but can reform, shattered units are gone for good. Flanking, being surrounded and cavalry charges work mainly by stacking morale penalties. In Warhammer III a unit can rout three times and shatters on the next break (forum and wiki sourced). Source: [ACOUP, Morale and Cohesion](https://acoup.blog/2022/07/01/collections-total-generalship-commanding-pre-modern-armies-part-iiic-morale-and-cohesion/); [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/totalwarwarhammer/leadership-and-unit-fatigue/z38a4f)

Darkest Dungeon (developer interview)
- Confirmed (Tyler Sigman and Chris Bourassa, Red Hook): each hero has a visible stress meter with numbers shown on every change. At 100 the hero takes a check. Failing is common and gives an Affliction. Passing gives a Virtue. The check is staged with a banner, a pause and a full screen result. Source: [Game Developer, Darkest Dungeon's Affliction System](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-darkest-dungeon-s-i-affliction-system)
- Confirmed: seven afflictions are named (selfish, abusive, hopeless, paranoid, afraid, masochistic, irrational) and five virtues (stalwart, courageous, focused, powerful, vigorous). Afflicted heroes act without the player: refuse healing, open trapped chests, pass turns. Each act out is usually led by a class specific bark and an icon over the head. The developers chose not to list every behaviour, so players learn by watching. Source: [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-darkest-dungeon-s-i-affliction-system)
- Confirmed goals: people break in different ways; the player is a squad leader with incomplete control; heroes should feel they have their own minds. Sigman said the inspiration was human experience more than other games. Source: [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-darkest-dungeon-s-i-affliction-system); [PC Gamer on the GDC talk](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-darkest-dungeon-got-inspiration-from-band-of-brothers-and-aliens); talk listing at [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023089/Darkest-Dungeon-A-Design)
- Fan sourced numbers: virtue chance has a floor of 1% and a cap of 60%, minus 5% per level the hero is under the dungeon. Source: [Darkest Dungeon wiki](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Virtue)

RimWorld (wiki)
- Mood under a threshold opens mental breaks in three bands. With the base threshold of 35%: minor at 35%, major at 20%, extreme at 5% (major is 4/7 of base, extreme is 1/7). Traits shift the threshold: Iron willed minus 18, Steadfast minus 9, Nervous plus 8. Breaks are not instant: mean time to a break is about 10 days in the minor band, 3 days in major, 0.7 days in extreme. Source: [RimWorld wiki, Mental Break Threshold](https://www.rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Mental_Break_Threshold)

Dwarf Fortress (wiki)
- A thought does not change mood directly. Each thought produces an emotion that depends on the dwarf's personality, and the emotion adds points to a running stress score that drifts back toward neutral. Strong emotions can be promoted to long term memory and are revisited later, adding stress again. Source: [Dwarf Fortress wiki, Stress](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/Stress); [Emotion](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Emotion)
- Personality facets (how a creature acts) are rated on a number scale and are separate from values (what it believes). Mid range facets are not shown in the profile. Stress is hidden from the player until it starts to bother the dwarf. Source: [Dwarf Fortress wiki, Personality facet](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Personality_facet); [Thoughts and preferences](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/Thoughts_and_preferences)

The Sims
- Described by AI and Games: a Sim has a set of needs, looks at the options around it and picks the one that fills its needs best. The Sims 3 replaced personality sliders with traits, and a trait works by adding more motives to the pile, not by weighting the scores. Source: [AI and Games transcript, The Genius AI Behind The Sims](https://amara.org/subtitles/cVoJS4OdmVql/en/1/download/The%20Genius%20AI%20Behind%20The%20Sims.en.txt)
- Confirmed (Richard Evans): each Sim can have up to five traits from a pool of about 80, and the stated goal was a personality clearly visible in autonomous behaviour. Source: [Evans, Representing Personality Traits as Conditionals](https://philarchive.org/archive/EVARPT); [GDC Vault, Modeling Individual Personalities in The Sims 3](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012294/Modeling-Individual-Personalities-in-The)

XCOM
- Player forum only: a soldier's chance to panic depends on the cause (an ally's death, a Muton's battle cry, an enemy psi attack) and the soldier's Will. Source: [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/200510/discussions/0/405692224235693721)

### Inferences
- Battle Brothers is the closest shipped model to Kindred Siege's nerve: a short ladder, one step per event, named triggers (ally falls, heavy wound, enemy leader falls), and weight by who fell and how close. Every part is integer and event driven. The roll against Bravery would use DeterministicRng, or be replaced by a pure threshold on a nerve integer so there is no roll at all.
- "Rally returns to Wavering, not Steady" is a hysteresis rule. It stops a fighter from flickering between states, and it makes a broken fighter visibly not the same as before. Total War's broken versus shattered split does the same job at army scale: one recoverable break, one final one.
- Darkest Dungeon splits the break by character: the same meter filling gives a different break per hero, and sometimes a heroic one. A nerve break that branches on the fighter's personality (flee, freeze, rage, protect a friend) and has a rare good branch is the highest value idea here for "iceberg depth that shows in the animation", because each branch is a different visible act.
- Darkest Dungeon stages the moment (banner, pause, splash) and leads each act out with a bark and an icon. That is the rule "the decision must show": one cue before, one act, one mark that stays. It fits the existing one blow at a time holds.
- RimWorld's bands show a second pattern: the state does not fire the instant the threshold is crossed, the risk climbs the deeper you go. In a deterministic sim this can be a countdown that shortens as nerve drops, which gives the player a visible warning window.
- Dwarf Fortress shows the cost of hiding: stress is invisible until it bites, so players need text to follow it. For a game where everything must show in animation, keep the deep numbers but always expose the current step of the ladder on the body.
- The Sims 3 lesson for personality in emotion: a trait is an extra motive, not a multiplier. A coward is a fighter with one more thing he wants (distance from the biggest enemy), which will show as movement.
- Leader effects are common to Total War and Battle Brothers: the leader steadies those near him and his death is the biggest single hit. This matches the Kindred Siege leader rules (leader off limits until his band falls) and gives the leader's fall a clear emotional job.
- Chain reactions need a cap. Battle Brothers ties each check to a single event and moves one step, which bounds the cascade. A nerve system where one break triggers checks on neighbours should move each neighbour at most one step per event.

### Gaps
- XCOM soldier panic rules (the Will test formula, whether panic chains) were not found in a reliable source. Only a forum post.
- No developer source reached for Total War's exact morale factors and weights. The manual gives the rules in words, not numbers.
- Darkest Dungeon: the heart attack at 200 stress and afflicted heroes stressing party members are well known but were not in the article I read. Not confirmed here.
- Dwarf Fortress: nothing here is attributed to Tarn Adams directly. The Game AI Pro 2 chapter by Adams on simulation principles was not reached.
- The Sims: the "advertising objects" part of the needs system was not confirmed in this pass.
- "Utility AI for emotions" as a named technique: no dedicated source found. The closest confirmed material is the utility section below.

## 4. Personality: trait driven utility per individual, and how it shows on screen

### Takeaway
The standard industry answer is utility scoring: every possible action gets a score from a handful of inputs passed through curves, and the best score wins. Personality is made by giving individuals or archetypes different action lists, curves and weights. It shows on screen only when the differing choice is a visibly different act.

### Cited Findings
- Confirmed (Mike Lewis, ArenaNet, Game AI Pro 3 chapter 13, read directly): in Guild Wars 2: Heart of Thorns each action an agent can take is a decision score evaluator (DSE). Each think cycle every DSE is scored and the best wins. Different archetypes or "species" can have very different sets of decisions. Source: [Game AI Pro 3, Choosing Effective Utility-Based Considerations (PDF)](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- Confirmed (same chapter): each consideration is a raw input normalised to 0 to 1 using designer set "bookends", then remapped through a response curve. The consideration scores are multiplied together to give the DSE score. Any consideration scoring zero vetoes the whole decision, and the system stops early, so cheap checks go first. Source: [Game AI Pro 3 ch. 13](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- Confirmed (same chapter): two standard considerations break repetition. "Runtime" drops a decision's score after it has run a set number of times, and "cooldown" forces it to zero until a delay has passed. A sequence of actions can be made just by cooldowns plus slightly lower weights on each next action. Source: [Game AI Pro 3 ch. 13](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- Confirmed (same chapter): to stop two close scoring decisions from flip flopping, give a "commitment bonus" to the last chosen decision, add weights so totals can exceed 1, and use runtime and cooldown. Always picking the absolute best score is "highly recommended". Source: [Game AI Pro 3 ch. 13](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro3/GameAIPro3_Chapter13_Choosing_Effective_Utility-Based_Considerations.pdf)
- Confirmed (same chapter): most response curves in the shipped game come from a small palette of presets, and most are monotonic because that is easier to reason about. Examples of considerations used: distance to target, own health (do not charge when near death, do not flank when badly hurt), whether the target is facing away (for a flank), danger of the area from an influence map. ### Inferences
- Utility scoring ports to integers cleanly. Use a fixed scale (for example 0 to 1000 for each consideration), response curves as small lookup tables (the chapter says shipped games use a small preset palette anyway), and multiply then divide by the scale with a fixed rounding rule. Ties break by a fixed action order. No floats, no dictionary iteration, same result on every machine.
- The zero veto is the cleanest place for hard personality rules ("this fighter never leaves his partner", "this one never retreats"). A trait can set one consideration to zero for one action.
- Personality per fighter can be three small things on top of a shared action list: a weight per action, a pick of preset curve per consideration, and one or two extra considerations (the Sims 3 "extra motive" idea). A reckless fighter uses a flat curve on own health for Charge. A careful one uses the steep drop the chapter describes.
- The commitment bonus and cooldowns matter more in an auto battler than in an MMO, because flip flopping reads as a bug when the player is only watching. A fighter should hold a choice long enough for its animation to finish.
- Nerve fits as one more consideration input. A wavering fighter's nerve value feeds the curves for Retreat, Guard and Charge. Then the morale ladder from section 3 and the personality weights here are one system, and the nerve break is simply the point where Flee out scores everything.
- Personality only shows if the losing option would have looked different. Two fighters choosing between targets at the same range show nothing. A fighter who steps back when the others step in shows everything. So pick the few decisions that have distinct animations (advance or hold, which target, protect or strike, flee or freeze) and put the personality weights there.
- The cue, act, mark pattern (Darkest Dungeon's bark and icon, the alien's visible doubt) is the on screen rule: a short tell before the personal choice, the act itself, and a small lasting sign. The suboptimal search trick says a little visible imperfection reads as thought.
- A chief's learned lessons can be the same data as a personality: each lesson is a weight change or an unlocked decision in the tribe's utility table. Then "what the chief learned" and "how the tribe fights" are one table the save file stores as integers.
