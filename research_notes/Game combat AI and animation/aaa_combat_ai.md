# AAA combat AI: decision architectures, group tactics, and what transfers to a deterministic 2D squad auto battler

Reading key for every finding below:
- **[DEV]** = stated by the developers themselves in a paper, talk or slides that I read in full.
- **[DEV, 2nd hand]** = attributed to a developer talk, but I only read a summary of it.
- **[COMMENT]** = a commentator's or dataminer's account, not confirmed by the studio.
- **[SNIPPET]** = I only saw a search result summary, the page body was not read. Treat as weak.

## 1. What architecture did each game use, and why?

### Takeaway
No well regarded game shipped a plain utility scorer on its own. The pattern that repeats is a small, strictly prioritised decision layer per unit (behaviour tree, HFSM, planner) sitting under a separate group layer that hands out roles, slots or orders, with a thin action layer that is almost nothing but "move" and "play an animation". The studios chose priority and structure over floating point scores on purpose, because scores became impossible to tune and made behaviour look random.

### Cited Findings

**Halo 2 (Bungie, Damian Isla, GDC 2005): behaviour tree, really a DAG, with binary relevance**
- [DEV] The core is a hierarchical state machine implemented as a behaviour DAG of about 50 behaviours. Children do not return a float desire, they return a binary "relevant or not". Source: [Handling Complexity in the Halo 2 AI](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- [DEV] Five decision schemes for a parent to pick a child: prioritised list (first relevant child wins, higher priority siblings can interrupt later, the most used scheme), sequential, sequential looping, probabilistic, and one off (never repeat a choice). Source: [Handling Complexity in the Halo 2 AI](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- [DEV] Why not utility floats: Isla says float desires become "almost impossible" to balance past two or three choices, and lists "murky behavior" (AI that seems random, not intentional) and loss of transparency as the failure. Source: [Handling Complexity in the Halo 2 AI](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- [DEV] Impulses: a trigger placed at a chosen priority in the tree that points at a behaviour somewhere else (for example "player in vehicle impulse"), so a low priority behaviour can jump the queue only under a named condition. Stimulus behaviours: an event (such as "leader died") inserts an impulse into the tree for one or two seconds only, so "flee when leader dies" is only checked right after the event. Source: [Handling Complexity in the Halo 2 AI](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- [DEV] Behaviour tagging: state such as alertness is a bit vector, and branches whose tags do not match are skipped, which locks and unlocks whole parts of the tree. Custom behaviours per character are spliced in at load (Grunts get many retreat impulses, Marines get coordination behaviours). Styles are allow or deny lists of behaviours plus parameter skews (a cowardly style flees sooner), kept in a vetted library because a bad style leaves a unit unable to act. Source: [Handling Complexity in the Halo 2 AI](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- [DEV] Tuning load: three parameters across about 115 behaviours and 30 character types would be about 10,350 numbers, so character files only store overrides and inherit the rest from a parent up to a generic root. Source: [Handling Complexity in the Halo 2 AI](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)

**Halo 3 (Bungie, Isla, GDC 2008): declarative objective trees above the behaviour tree**
- [DEV] Control stack: encounter logic, then task, then squad. "Designer provides the strategic intelligence", and "within the task, the AI behaves autonomously". The stated goal is "the illusion of strategic intelligence". Source: [Building a Better Battle, slides](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)
- [DEV] Halo 2 gave designers an FSM tool with explicit transitions ("< 75% alive?"), which grew as n squared. Halo 3 replaced it with a tree of prioritised tasks, each self describing with a priority, an activation condition and a capacity. Squads are poured in at the top and filter down to the most important open tasks: "Basically, it's a plinko machine." Tasks switch themselves on and off, squads are pulled up when a higher task activates and pushed down when theirs closes. Source: [Building a Better Battle, slides](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)
- [DEV] The canonical encounter is a two stage fallback: hold territory, pushed to a fallback point, pushed to a last stand, then the player "breaks" them. Example conditions on the slides: forward while more than 75% alive, fallback while more than 50%, then last stand, with a cap of 10 per generator. Source: [Building a Better Battle, slides](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)
- [DEV] Assignment is greedy: repeatedly take the (squad, task) pair with the lowest cost H(s,t) that fits the capacity. Cost terms named: do not travel far, act coordinated, keep the tree balanced, get near the player. Filters ("must be a sniper", "must be in a vehicle") are an infinite cost. Source: [Building a Better Battle, slides](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)

**F.E.A.R. (Monolith, Jeff Orkin, GDC 2006): GOAP planner over a three state FSM**
- [DEV] The FSM has three states, Goto, Animate and UseSmartObject, and the last is a data driven Animate, so really two: move somewhere, play an animation. All logic about which and when lives in the planner. Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)
- [DEV] Goals compete for activation by priority. The planner runs A* over actions (each with preconditions, effects and a cost) to find the cheapest sequence that satisfies the winning goal. Differences from STRIPS: a cost per action, no add and delete lists, procedural preconditions and effects. Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)
- [DEV] Why: the previous games (NOLF2, Tron 2.0) had goals with embedded state machines that became "overly complex, bloated, unmanageable". With planning, each character type gets its own action set and "we never have to manually specify the transitions". Behaviour is layered by adding a goal or action: dodge (DodgeShuffle, DodgeRoll), then cover, DodgeCovered, BlindFireFromCover, then an Ambush goal when cover is invalidated, then dialogue. Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)
- [DEV] Replanning from failure: the soldier tries the door, fails, records that in working memory, replans to kick it, fails, replans to dive through the window and ends close enough to melee. Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)

**Killzone 3 (Guerrilla, Straatman, Verweij, Champandard and others): three layer HTN**
- [DEV] Three layers: commander (per faction, knows the game mode), squad, individual. Orders move down (Attack area, Defend area, Escort player, Advance to regroup point) and information moves up (success, or imminent failure). Source: [Hierarchical AI for Multiplayer Bots in Killzone 3, Game AI Pro ch. 29](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter29_Hierarchical_AI_for_Multiplayer_Bots_in_Killzone_3.pdf)
- [DEV] Individuals and squads both run a custom HTN planner, chosen "because it provides good control over what plans can be generated and a clear definition of priorities between plans". A domain is methods, each a list of branches with a precondition and a task list, tried in written order from a root task called behave, so self preservation branches are considered before healing friends. Source: [Game AI Pro ch. 29](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter29_Hierarchical_AI_for_Multiplayer_Bots_in_Killzone_3.pdf)
- [DEV] The agent replans at a fixed rate and swaps plan only if the new one uses branches higher up the list than the current one (grenade nearby beats healing a buddy). "Continuation condition" branches hold a single continue task that tells the planner the current plan is still best, which stops needless plan churn. A failed task invalidates the whole plan. Source: [Game AI Pro ch. 29](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter29_Hierarchical_AI_for_Multiplayer_Bots_in_Killzone_3.pdf)
- [DEV] The squad does not act, it only puts orders in members' command queues. One order (DefendMarker) decomposes differently by class: engineer "move there, place a turret nearby", tactician "move there, call in a sentry drone", generic "move there, scan around". Source: [Game AI Pro ch. 29](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter29_Hierarchical_AI_for_Multiplayer_Bots_in_Killzone_3.pdf)

**The Last of Us (Naughty Dog, Travis McIntosh): prioritised skill FSM plus behaviour stack plus spatial scoring**
- [DEV] Top level states are "skills" in an FSM. Skills are prioritised, each asks every frame whether it wants to run, the highest priority wins. Examples: panic, advance, melee, gun combat, hide, investigate, scripted, flank. Each skill has its own small state machine and pushes simple behaviours (MoveToLocation, StandAndShoot, TakeCover) onto a behaviour stack. Source: [Human Enemy AI in The Last of Us, Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] Where to stand is the one place they use scoring. "Post selectors" (17 shipped) each list criteria; every criterion returns 0 to 1, the values are multiplied, the top post wins. All selectors are evaluated continuously, so the ideal post for any skill is always ready when the unit switches state. Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] "Spatial analysis of the play space was perhaps the biggest win for us in terms of improving the AI's decision making", and half of the implementation decisions came from watching videos of focus testers. Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)

**Alien: Isolation (Creative Assembly): director plus behaviour tree**
- [COMMENT, with developer input] Two layers. A director always knows where the player is but only hints the alien toward an area. The alien must find the player with its own senses and "is never allowed to cheat". The alien is a behaviour tree of over 100 nodes with about 30 top level nodes, and branches unlock as the player meets conditions so it seems to learn. Source: [The Perfect Organism, Tommy Thompson](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)
- [COMMENT] The director tracks a menace gauge (rises when the alien is near, in sight, or close on the tracker). At peak it sends the alien backstage into vents to give the player a break. A job system gives the alien tasks with priorities that decide whether it finishes, blends or interrupts its current action. Source: [The Perfect Organism](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)
- [DEV, 2nd hand] Andy Bray (Creative Assembly) is credited with the "psychopathic serendipity" aim, the rule that unlocks never come from player deaths, and that the alien teleports only twice in the campaign, both for cutscenes. The node counts and gauge details are not individually attributed to him. Source: [The Perfect Organism](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)

**Doom 2016 (id Software): HFSM plus animation state machine**
- [COMMENT] Hierarchical finite state machines inherited from Rage. Designers assemble behaviours from pre built states, and allowed transitions are fixed in code. "AnimWeb" is a state machine of animations that handles blending. 16 archetypes. Source: [Cyber Demons: The AI of DOOM (2016), Tommy Thompson](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)
- [COMMENT] One jump animation is adjusted at run time for short and long leaps, and focus tracking turns head, chest and hips so demons always face the player. The author says the token, cover and editor details came from unnamed sources, not from the cited GDC talks. Source: [Cyber Demons](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)

**God of War 2018 (Santa Monica Studio, Mihir Sheth, GDC 2019)**
- [DEV, 2nd hand, machine made summary] Enemies are either aggressive (attack actively) or non aggressive (wait for openings). An aggression score picks who is aggressive from four factors: potential aggressiveness, a designer set priority, whether the player is targeting that enemy, and the enemy's rank relative to Kratos. Source: [summary of "Evolving Combat in God of War for a New Perspective"](https://videohighlight.com/v/hE5tWF-Ou2k); the talk itself: [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026423/Evolving-Combat-in-God-of)

**Sekiro and Elden Ring (FromSoftware)**
- [SNIPPET, COMMENT] Community decompiles describe the AI as a stack of "Goals" (a pushdown automaton) written in Havok Script, a Lua variant, where an attack goal is mostly parameterised by an animation ID and most logic sits in each goal's "activate" function. Source: [The Low Tech AI of Elden Ring, summary](https://feedbagel.com/post/the-low-tech-ai-of-elden-ring); tooling: [DSLuaDecompiler](https://github.com/nex3/DSLuaDecompiler)
- [SNIPPET, COMMENT] Dataminer Zullie the Witch says enemies react when the player's animation starts (for example the heal), so they read game state, not raw button input, and she admits "some amount of conjecture". Source: [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/heres-why-elden-ring-enemies-stab-you-every-time-you-try-to-heal); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/elden-ring-dataminer-explains-why-enemies-smack-you-when-you-reach-for-a-potion)

**Total War (Creative Assembly)**
- [COMMENT] Shogun: two layers, a campaign AI and a battle AI that commands groups of units, with each unit switching between several small pre trained neural networks by objective (hold formation, avoid fire, take an attack position); nothing learns during play. Source: [80.lv summary of Thompson's piece](https://80.lv/articles/breakdown-the-ai-of-total-war)
- [SNIPPET, DEV blog] A "Grand Tactical Analyser" decides whether the whole alliance attacks or defends, an Entry Points Manager sits under it, and invisible "hint lines" around hills tell the battle AI the hill is worth holding. Source: [totalwar.com blog](https://totalwar.com/?p=13725)
- [SNIPPET, DEV paper] A learned combat outcome model feeds the auto resolver and informs whether battle AI units engage or retreat. Source: [Learning Combat Outcomes in Creative Assembly's Total War, AIIDE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/36830)

### Inferences
- The shared shape is three layers: (1) a group brain that owns roles, slots and orders, (2) a per unit priority structure that decides what to do given its role, (3) an action layer that is only "go there" and "play this clip". F.E.A.R. states layer 3 outright, and it is the layer that guarantees every decision shows in animation, because a decision can only ever come out as a move or a clip.
- Why "a plain utility scorer" reads as basic: Halo 2's authors rejected float desires for exactly the two symptoms the owner would see, random looking behaviour and untunable numbers. The studios that do score (The Last of Us posts, Halo 3 H(s,t)) score only the "where" or "who", never the "what". The "what" is a strict priority order.
- Everything here is deterministic friendly. Prioritised lists, binary relevance, HTN branch order, greedy assignment with a fixed tie break, and integer weights need no floats and no random numbers. The Halo 2 "probabilistic" and "one off" schemes would use the sim's DeterministicRng. The Last of Us post score (floats multiplied) would become integer per mille products or a lexicographic compare.
- Planning (GOAP, HTN) earned its place where a unit must chain steps toward a goal with obstacles in the way. A lane based auto battler with a handful of moves per class probably does not need A* over actions. What transfers is the idea, not the search: a short, authored, class specific decomposition of one squad order (Killzone's DefendMarker becoming three different class plans) and replanning only when a higher branch becomes valid.
- Iceberg depth candidates that are cheap to build: Halo 2 stimulus impulses (a two second window after "ally died", "leader parried", "mark cashed" in which special reactions become legal), behaviour tags that lock and unlock branches by nerve or alert state, and styles per tribe as allow or deny lists plus skews over one shared tree.

### Gaps
- Killzone 2's own squad HTN talk (Guerrilla, Paris Game AI Conference and GDC) was not read. The Killzone 3 chapter is from the same team and describes the same family of system, but numbers such as plans per second are not in the text I read.
- The Last of Us Part II: no architecture source read. A GDC talk exists ([Melee AI in The Last of Us Part II, Allen Chou](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027115/contactUs)) but only the listing was reachable.
- Doom Eternal: nothing specific found beyond Doom 2016.
- Batman Arkham: no developer account of the enemy decision architecture was found. Only trade press snippets (see section 2).
- Ghost of Tsushima: the GDC talks found ([Honoring the Blade](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027184/Honoring-the-Blade-Lethality-and), [Master of the Katana](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027019/Master-of-the-Katana-Melee)) are about lethality and melee design. I could not read them and found no account of the enemy AI architecture.
- Sekiro and Elden Ring: FromSoftware has published nothing I could find. Everything is from decompiles, and I only saw summaries.
- God of War: the only detail is from a machine generated summary of the talk. Needs checking against the recording before anyone relies on it.
- Total War: I did not find or read a primary source for the modern battle AI (detachments, tactics, siege). The neural network claim is about Shogun era games and is second hand.

## 2. How did they coordinate groups?

### Takeaway
Almost every game uses a central referee that individuals must ask before attacking, flanking or taking a role, and the referee says yes to very few at a time. Flanking and pincers were mostly not coded as tactics. They fall out of slot assignment, cover choice and a path cost, and then barks make them look planned.

### Cited Findings

**The kung fu circle and its replacement (Kingdoms of Amalur: Reckoning, Michael Dawe)**
- [DEV] The plain kung fu circle is "opponents attack the player one at a time", judged "too strict for a game with quick flowing combat". Letting "two or three" attack ignores enemy type and attack strength. Source: [Beyond the Kung-Fu Circle, Game AI Pro ch. 28](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter28_Beyond_the_Kung-Fu_Circle_A_Flexible_System_for_Managing_NPC_Attacks.pdf)
- [DEV] "Belgian AI": the target carries an eight slot grid plus two budgets, grid capacity and attack capacity. Every creature has a grid weight, every attack an attack weight. Worked example: grid capacity 12, soldier weight 4, troll weight 8, so one soldier plus one troll fill it and a second soldier must wait outside. Attack capacity 10: troll charge costs 6 (club 4), leaving 4, so the soldier cannot use his lunge (5) and settles for a sword swing (3). Source: [Game AI Pro ch. 28](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter28_Beyond_the_Kung-Fu_Circle_A_Flexible_System_for_Managing_NPC_Attacks.pdf)
- [DEV] A central "stage manager" owns all positioning; individuals hold no spatial reasoning and re ask for their slot every frame. Three rings: waiting outside, an approach circle for those with a slot, an attack circle only for those launching an attack, which "helps the player to determine which creatures are immediate melee threats". Source: [Game AI Pro ch. 28](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter28_Beyond_the_Kung-Fu_Circle_A_Flexible_System_for_Managing_NPC_Attacks.pdf)
- [DEV] Rules that made it read well: anyone inside the outer circle without permission must leave as fast as possible; waiting creatures stand in front of an empty slot, "which made for natural flanking behavior without any creature explicitly needing to know about any other creature"; a creature gives its slot back right after attacking so attackers rotate and "no single creature could monopolize attack opportunities"; an attacker locks its slot during the attack so the budget cannot be overrun; the manager can steal a slot for a better placed creature. Source: [Game AI Pro ch. 28](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter28_Beyond_the_Kung-Fu_Circle_A_Flexible_System_for_Managing_NPC_Attacks.pdf)
- [DEV] Difficulty is two numbers: raise grid capacity (more can surround) and attack capacity (more attack at once, with heavier attacks) without touching any creature's weights. Cooldowns per individual, per creature type and global stop the same attack being spammed. Source: [Game AI Pro ch. 28](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter28_Beyond_the_Kung-Fu_Circle_A_Flexible_System_for_Managing_NPC_Attacks.pdf)

**Doom 2016 attack tokens**
- [COMMENT, unnamed sources] Each attack type (melee, ranged, charged) has a limited token pool that varies by difficulty. A demon requests a token before attacking and returns it after. A better placed demon can steal a token so front line demons do not stand idle. No new attack can start during a glory kill. Source: [Cyber Demons](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)

**God of War 2018**
- [DEV, 2nd hand, machine made summary] Only two enemies can be aggressive at once. An aggressive enemy that gets interrupted keeps its token briefly, so the passive ones cannot jump in during the gap. Higher difficulty changes the enemy side token counts. Aggressive enemies take a crescent in front of Kratos, non aggressive ones a circle around him, and back line enemies are kept visible behind the front line. An early weighted circular grid that moved with Kratos was dropped in favour of "prevent bad positions, do not optimise placement". Source: [summary of Sheth's GDC 2019 talk](https://videohighlight.com/v/hE5tWF-Ou2k)

**The Last of Us combat coordinator (roles as tickets)**
- [DEV] A global Combat Coordinator manages named roles: Flanker, Approacher, Investigator, StayUpAndAimer, OpportunisticShooter. An NPC calls RequestRole(); if free it gets it and nobody else can until release. Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] Two assignment rules: first come first served where speed matters (OpportunisticShooter exists so that at least one NPC is always shooting, and it interrupts whatever the NPC was doing, even mid animation), and best candidate only where quality matters (each NPC rates its flank route by cost every frame, only the ideal Flanker's request is granted). Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] "It was only necessary for one NPC to be shooting the player at any given time; all other NPCs could spend their time taking cover, flanking, etc." Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] Flanking: the "combat vector" is the direction of combat from the player's view, the average of NPC positions weighted by recent shots. Path cost rises the closer a route is to that centre line, so flank paths swing wide and come from behind, "which was precisely what the player expected". Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)

**F.E.A.R. squads**
- [DEV] A global coordinator periodically re clusters AI into squads by proximity; each squad runs zero or one squad behaviour. Four simple ones shipped: Get to Cover (one suppresses while the rest reach valid cover), Advance Cover (same, but to cover nearer the threat), Orderly Advance (single file, each covers the one ahead, the last faces backwards), Search (pairs that cover each other room by room). Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)
- [DEV] A squad behaviour has four steps: find members to fill its slots, send orders, monitor each tick, end on success or failure. Orders are just one more goal for the individual, so "fleeing from danger may trump following an order". Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)
- [DEV] "We actually did not have any complex squad behaviors at all in F.E.A.R." Flanks and pincers were side effects: a soldier ordered to the only valid cover takes a back route and "it appears that the A.I. is flanking"; Advance Cover with obstacles in the way looks like "some kind of coordinated pincher attack". Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)

**Halo 2 and 3**
- [DEV] Halo 2: squads get orders that point at groups of firing positions, and orders switch on triggers such as how many squad members have died. Source: [Handling Complexity in the Halo 2 AI](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- [DEV] Halo 3 leadership: a core task takes only leaders, peripheral tasks take only followers; when the leader dies the task enters a "broken" state that freezes redistribution and the followers play "broken" behaviours. "Leader death breaks followers." Source: [Building a Better Battle, slides](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)

**Killzone 3**
- [DEV] Squads plan on a strategic graph (areas summarising the waypoint graph) plus a per faction influence map built from counts of friendly and enemy bots, turrets and recent deaths, smoothed over distance and time. Squad paths avoid enemy held areas, and areas already chosen by a friendly squad get a penalty so repeated attacks vary their route. Source: [Game AI Pro ch. 29](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter29_Hierarchical_AI_for_Multiplayer_Bots_in_Killzone_3.pdf)

**Batman Arkham**
- [SNIPPET, trade interview] Arkham Asylum's thugs mostly came one at a time; Arkham City let them attack together to raise pressure. AI programmer Tim Hanagan's team is credited with the group fight AI. Source: [Copcomm trade issue](https://digital.copcomm.com/i/48986/47)
- [COMMENT] On Easy and Normal a marker appears over an enemy's head just before it attacks, the cue to counter. Source: [StrategyWiki, Arkham Asylum combat](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Batman:_Arkham_Asylum/Combat)

### Inferences
- Tokens, tickets, roles and slots are one idea: a shared budget held by a referee. For a deterministic sim this is ideal, because it is a few integers in ordered state, with requests resolved in a fixed unit order.
- The weighted version (Amalur) is the strongest fit for a squad of five against a band. Give each enemy a weight and each move a weight, give the player's squad (or each of our units) a capacity. One big hit plus one small hit can be in flight, never two big ones. That is also a direct lever on the parry: the leader should only ever have one parryable big hit to read at a time, which matches the game's existing "one parryable at a time" rule.
- In an auto battler both sides are AI, so the budget can apply both ways. Our own five can use roles the same way the Last of Us enemies do: one "OpportunisticShooter" style role guarantees someone is always pressing, one "layer" lays the mark, one "casher" is granted the cash. Role granted to the best candidate only (like Flanker) is how to stop two classes cashing the same mark.
- "Keep the token briefly after an interrupt" (God of War, second hand) is a small rule with big feel: when our leader parries a big hit, the parried enemy still holds the slot for a beat, so the squad gets a clean window instead of the next enemy filling the gap at once.
- Rotation matters as much as the cap. Amalur's "give the slot back right after attacking" and the "leave the inner ring if you have no permission" rule are what make the crowd look like it is taking turns on purpose. In 2.5D with fixed spots this maps to a step forward to strike and a step back to the waiting row, both of which are clips the art already needs.
- Flanking can be faked the cheap way the studios did it: assign waiting units to the empty slot in front of them, or give each unit a path cost that avoids the centre line. Nobody needs a "flank" tactic.
- Halo 3's "broken" state is close to this game's nerve break and to the rule that the enemy leader retreats when his band falls. The source backs the design: leaders give the encounter structure, and their death visibly breaks followers.

### Gaps
- No developer source read for Arkham's attack scheduling, for Ghost of Tsushima's (commonly described as enemies waiting their turn in a ring), for Sekiro or Elden Ring group behaviour, or for Assassin's Creed and Spider-Man, which are often cited for the same pattern.
- Doom's token details are from a commentator's unnamed sources. The id Software GDC talk ([Embracing Push Forward Combat in DOOM](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024940/Embracing-Push-Forward-Combat-in)) was not read to confirm them.
- No numbers for God of War token counts or radii.
- Total War formation and flank logic: nothing primary found.

## 3. Perception and memory

### Takeaway
The good systems separate what is true from what each unit believes, add a short build up before a unit "knows" something, and share knowledge through explicit messages. Being believably wrong, and then visibly finding out, is what reads as a mind.

### Cited Findings
- [DEV] The Last of Us awareness timer: seeing the player starts a timer that counts up each frame seen and down each frame unseen. The player counts as perceived at about 1 or 2 seconds for a typical NPC, much less in combat, much more if the NPC has never perceived the player. Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] The Last of Us vision: a simple cone failed in playtests (close players unseen, far ones noticed). Fix: view angle inversely proportional to distance. The visibility test went from weighted rays to nearly every joint (threshold typically 60%) down to one point, on the chest in stealth and on top of the head in combat, because players could not predict the complex version. Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] The Last of Us last known position and sharing: "the NPCs did not cheat with regard to knowing the player's location in most circumstances". On perceiving, an NPC creates an entity with location and time stamp and signals all other NPCs. If nobody perceives the player the location is never updated. Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] The Last of Us search map: grid cells of where the player could be. Known position means one cell; once lost, active cells bleed into neighbours over time, and any cell an NPC can currently see is cleared. NPCs search what is left. Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] The Last of Us combat cycle: surround, advance, and if the player is unseen for 10 seconds or more one NPC is chosen to go and check. Focus tests showed the cycle took nearly 2 minutes. "Our solution to this problem was to cheat": if the player moves more than 5 m from where they think he is, one NPC is sent to check at once, which cut the cycle to about 30 seconds. Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] Halo 2 props: the AI's world model stores believed state separately from true state, so the AI can be "tricked" or "surprised". Isla calls it rudimentary: a flat list of potential targets with no spatial relations. Memory is in four kinds: per behaviour persistent, per behaviour short term, per object, and per object per behaviour. Source: [Handling Complexity in the Halo 2 AI](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- [DEV] F.E.A.R.: sensors keep each AI's list of nearby valid cover up to date, so the squad layer never analyses the map, it only picks from what members already know. Facts learned from failure (the blocked door) go in working memory and shape the next plan. Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)
- [DEV] Killzone 3: sensors for seeing, hearing and feeling with view cones and maximum distances; threats go in each bot's own world database, and "because of the limitations in perception, the bot's idea of the world may be believably wrong". Squads build their world state only from members' messages, not from perception. Source: [Game AI Pro ch. 29](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter29_Hierarchical_AI_for_Multiplayer_Bots_in_Killzone_3.pdf)
- [COMMENT] Alien: Isolation senses: footsteps, gunshots, the motion tracker at roughly 1.5 m, and short ray traces behind the alien so players cannot shadow it. It visits search points in a deliberately sub optimal order and backtracks, which reads as searching. Designers can block spots it never looks at, to avoid unfair kills. Source: [The Perfect Organism](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)
- [SNIPPET, DEV interviews] The Last of Us Part II: every human enemy gets a name at spawn, enemies call each other by name when coordinating (flank, search that building), pass on where Ellie was seen, and react in anguish when a named friend dies. Source: [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/the-last-of-us-2s-upgraded-enemy-ai-will-make-for-/1100-6470119/); [PlayStation Universe](https://psu.com/news/the-human-threat-in-the-last-of-us-part-ii-will-know-each-other-by-name)
- [DEV, 2nd hand, machine made summary] God of War: off screen enemies keep their last known quadrant relative to Kratos, and off screen aggressive enemies do not come back in until the player has acknowledged them. Red arrows mark incoming attacks, white arrows idle enemies. Source: [summary of Sheth's GDC 2019 talk](https://videohighlight.com/v/hE5tWF-Ou2k)

### Inferences
- An auto battler has no stealth, but "belief separate from truth" still applies to things a unit could know about the fight: who it thinks is the biggest threat, whether it has noticed a mark on its ally, whether it has "seen" that the enemy leader parries. A unit that reacts a beat late, turns its head, then commits, shows its decision in animation. That is the same awareness timer, counted in sim ticks.
- Integer form: an awareness counter per (unit, fact) that goes up by a fixed amount per tick while the cause holds and down otherwise, with a threshold per class and a lower threshold in the heat of a fight. Fully deterministic.
- Information sharing as explicit messages gives free visible beats: the unit that first notices plays a point or shout clip, and only then do the others' beliefs update. The Last of Us and Killzone both route knowledge through messages, not shared global truth.
- Reaction time is a designer dial, not realism. Naughty Dog shortened it in combat and lengthened it for the unaware, and cheated outright (the 5 m rule) when the honest system was slow. For a fast roguelike fight the lesson is to keep noticing delays short and visible, and to cut any honest delay that playtests show as dead air.
- Memory of failure (F.E.A.R. door) is cheap depth: "my last blade hit was stopped by that shield, so I switch target or wait for the mark" needs one remembered fact per unit, and it produces a visible change of plan.

### Gaps
- No sourced numbers for Halo's perception or reaction delays, or for FromSoftware games.
- The Last of Us Part II details are from press interviews seen as search summaries only.
- The God of War off screen rules need checking against the real talk.

## 4. Tricks for perceived intelligence

### Takeaway
The studios say openly that the goal is the illusion. The strongest tricks are to say the decision out loud, to make intent visible before the act, and to hide or excuse inaction. Several famous "smart" moments were never implemented at all.

### Cited Findings
- [DEV] F.E.A.R.: "There is no point in spending time and effort implementing squad behaviors if in the end the coordination of the A.I. is not apparent to the player." The squad layer picks a dialogue line after it has already decided what the AI will do. Players then believed the soldiers were giving and obeying orders: "it's all smoke and mirrors". Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)
- [DEV] F.E.A.R.: "Vocalizing intentions can sometimes even be enough, without any actual implementation." The last survivor says he needs reinforcements; nothing calls any, but the next enemies the player meets are assumed to be them. Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)
- [DEV] F.E.A.R.: prefer an exchange between two characters over one announcement (one asks status, the hurt one answers), and use lines to explain a lack of action: a soldier under fire with no better position says "I've got nowhere to go!" so he looks stuck, not stupid. Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)
- [DEV] The Last of Us: "Dialog in particular allowed the AI to communicate the decisions they make to the player, so that the NPCs could appear to be as intelligent as they sometimes were." Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] The Last of Us lethality: every shot the player takes plays a full body hit reaction that removes control for a moment, "a punctuation mark". The stated design question was how to make enemies real enough that the player feels bad killing them, so enemies had to be dangerous, coordinate "or at least appear to coordinate", and care about their own safety. Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] Halo: the Bungie talk is titled "The Illusion of Intelligence" and the speakers stress it is not "The Reality of Intelligence". Source: [Butcher and Griesemer, GDC 2002, transcript](https://halo.bungie.org/misc/gdc.2002.haloai/talk.html?page=2)
- [SNIPPET, 2nd hand] From summaries of that talk: players had more fun when they could see what state the AI was in, and an AI may have abilities the player lacks if the ability is recognisable and its limits are predictable. Source: [blog summary of the talk](https://henryfinalproject.blogspot.com/2019/04/the-illusion-of-intelligence-talk-by.html)
- [DEV] Halo 3: "The dance is about the illusion of strategic intelligence", supplied by the designer. Leaders give structure and their death visibly breaks followers. Source: [Building a Better Battle, slides](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)
- [DEV] Amalur: positioning itself is a telegraph. Only creatures about to attack may enter the inner ring, so the player can read who is a threat from where they stand. Source: [Game AI Pro ch. 28](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter28_Beyond_the_Kung-Fu_Circle_A_Flexible_System_for_Managing_NPC_Attacks.pdf)
- [COMMENT] Doom 2016 deliberate imperfection: demon accuracy uses a weighted spread, they miss more when the player moves fast, and they will not shoot explosive barrels near the player. Pain builds in visible steps from twitch to falter to stagger, with specific weapons causing specific reactions. Source: [Cyber Demons](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)
- [COMMENT, with developer input] Alien: Isolation: the director hints, the alien "finds". Branch unlocks over the campaign make it seem to learn, and never from player deaths. It is pulled backstage when menace peaks. Source: [The Perfect Organism](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)
- [SNIPPET, COMMENT] Elden Ring enemies punish a heal because they react to the start of the player's animation, which players read as the enemy reading their mind (or as unfair). Source: [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/heres-why-elden-ring-enemies-stab-you-every-time-you-try-to-heal)

### Inferences
- The owner's rule "every decision shows in the animation" is the same rule the studios arrived at from the other side: a decision the player cannot see was wasted work (F.E.A.R.), and visible state made the game more fun (Halo). The practical form is a contract: no decision may change behaviour unless it also emits a visible beat (a glance, a shout word, a stance shift, a step into the strike row) that starts before the act.
- Decide first, announce second, act third. F.E.A.R. chooses the bark after the squad decision. In a deterministic sim the decision tick can be recorded in the event log, and the screen can spend its telegraph frames on it. At 20 frames per second a two or three frame anticipation pose (100 to 150 ms) is the smallest readable tell, and the sim can commit the decision that many ticks early so the result is unchanged.
- Explaining inaction is the cheapest win for an auto battler, where idle units are the main thing that looks dumb. A unit refused a token should not stand still. It should play a "waiting my turn" behaviour: circle, feint, guard, taunt, or a short word. Amalur moves refused units out of the ring on purpose, and F.E.A.R. gives the stuck soldier a line.
- Fake depth is allowed and normal. The reinforcements line cost nothing to build. For this game, a unit shouting for a mark just before an ally lays one, when the sim had already scheduled it, will read as teamwork.
- Paired beats read as smarter than solo ones (F.E.A.R.'s dialogue rule). A mark laid and cashed is already a pair. Showing the layer signal and the casher answer makes the existing rule look like a conversation.
- Deliberate imperfection has to be visible and fair. Doom's misses scale with the player's speed, and Naughty Dog simplified the vision test because players could not predict the accurate one. Predictable limits beat accurate simulation.
- The iceberg is authored variety on a small, legible surface, not a cleverer scorer: Halo 2's roughly 50 behaviours times styles times per character impulses, Alien's 100 plus nodes that unlock over time, and F.E.A.R.'s layers of goals. Depth came from many small rules with clear priority, each with its own clip or line.

### Gaps
- The Halo 2002 talk's often quoted playtest result (tougher enemies were rated as smarter) was not confirmed. I only reached the intro page of the transcript, so I have not listed it as a finding. The full transcript runs 27 pages at the URL above.
- No primary source read on telegraph timing numbers (wind up frames) for Sekiro, Arkham, God of War or Ghost of Tsushima. Another researcher's animation notes may cover this.
- No source read for Left 4 Dead's director, which is the usual reference for pacing by a stress gauge.

## 5. Known failure modes and what the studios did about them

### Takeaway
The failures are the same across studios: dithering, units standing idle, plans going stale, coordination nobody notices, honest systems that feel slow, and tools that designers cannot reason about. The fixes are mostly small rules, not new architectures.

### Cited Findings
- [DEV] Dithering (flipping between two actions): Halo 2 names it and notes a bonus for the previous winner as the standard fix; its own answer was binary relevance and prioritised lists so there are no near ties to flip between. Source: [Handling Complexity in the Halo 2 AI](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- [DEV] Untunable scores and "murky" random looking behaviour: Halo 2 dropped float desires, and manages parameters by inheritance. Source: [Handling Complexity in the Halo 2 AI](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2005-proceeding-handling-complexity-in-the-i-halo-2-i-ai)
- [DEV] Transition explosion: Halo 2's designer FSMs grew as n squared; Halo 3 moved to self describing prioritised tasks. Its own "badness" list: needs designer training, an awkward fit with the scripting system, tying allied and enemy fronts together was complicated, and the squad was not always the right unit to assign (a lone unit handed a sniper rifle could not take a sniper task). The slides also warn "AI can look really stupid with wrong H(f)". Source: [Building a Better Battle, slides](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~rich/courses/imgd4000-d09/lectures/halo3.pdf)
- [DEV] State machine bloat: Monolith's NOLF2 goal FSMs became "a risk to the stability of the project", fixed by planning with per character action sets. Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)
- [DEV] Orders overridden by self interest: a F.E.A.R. squad behaviour simply fails if a member flees instead (a grenade invalidates the cover), and the squad layer picks again. The dialogue system was separate from the planner and hand hooked, which produced wrong lines, such as asking "What's your status?" of an ally who had just been blown apart. Source: [Three States and a Plan](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf)
- [DEV] Stale or churning plans: Killzone 3 replans at a fixed rate, replaces a plan only with one from a higher branch, uses continuation conditions to keep a good plan, and drops plans that lost relevance (the buddy to heal has died). Commander squad assignment was "much harder to get working" with more objectives and fewer bots, which pushed them to an HTN based commander. Source: [Game AI Pro ch. 29](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter29_Hierarchical_AI_for_Multiplayer_Bots_in_Killzone_3.pdf)
- [DEV] Slot systems going stale: in Amalur a creature's assigned slot stopped being the nearest after the player rolled away. Fix: the stage manager owns all assignments, creatures never remember a slot and re ask every frame, the manager can steal and shift slots to cut total travel, and attackers lock their slot so capacity is never overrun. Same attack spam at high difficulty was fixed with cooldowns at three scopes. Source: [Game AI Pro ch. 28](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter28_Beyond_the_Kung-Fu_Circle_A_Flexible_System_for_Managing_NPC_Attacks.pdf)
- [DEV] Too slow to threaten: Last of Us NPCs were "noticeably slow in transitioning to shooting", so rushing players went untouched. Fix: the OpportunisticShooter role interrupts anything, even mid animation. Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] Looks dumb while moving: Last of Us NPCs ran toward the player to reach the "best" cover. Fix: a criterion that rejects cover whose path runs toward the player for long (paths refreshed about every half second). Flank routes from the exposure map "could vary wildly from one frame to the next". Fix: the stable combat vector cost shape. Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [DEV] Honest but boring: the Last of Us lost player cycle of nearly 2 minutes was cut to about 30 seconds by cheating (the 5 m rule). Source: [Game AI Pro 2 ch. 34](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)
- [COMMENT] Front line demons standing idle while a token sat with a badly placed demon: Doom 2016 lets demons steal tokens. A planned minigun perpetual falter on the Cacodemon was cut before launch. The designer editor's extra abstraction made run time debugging harder. Source: [Cyber Demons](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-)
- [DEV, 2nd hand, machine made summary] God of War: a weighted grid that moved with Kratos was abandoned for simple zone constraints, and players felt anxious about enemies they could not see, which led to quadrant memory, "do not re enter until acknowledged", and arrows. Source: [summary of Sheth's GDC 2019 talk](https://videohighlight.com/v/hE5tWF-Ou2k)
- [COMMENT] Unfair learning: Alien: Isolation never unlocks behaviour from player deaths, and never teleports the alien outside two cutscenes. Source: [The Perfect Organism](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)
- [SNIPPET] Kung fu circle backlash: a Steam thread calls Ghost of Tsushima's enemy AI passive (player opinion only). Source: [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2215430/discussions/0/6579276631059901577)

### Inferences
- Failure list to design against, each with its proven fix:
  1. Dithering: strict priority order, a commit window (a chosen move cannot be dropped until its clip reaches a cancel frame), and a plan swap only for a strictly higher branch (Killzone).
  2. Idle units: a "no token" behaviour set that is itself animated, plus token stealing by the better placed unit (Doom, Amalur).
  3. Token overrun and interrupts: lock while attacking, hold briefly after an interrupt (Amalur, God of War).
  4. Sameness: cooldowns at unit, type and global scope, and Halo 2's "one off" scheme so a unit never repeats its last pick.
  5. Invisible smarts: no decision without a tell or a word (F.E.A.R.).
  6. Wrong word at the wrong time: drive words from the same decision record as the move, not from a separate hook (F.E.A.R.'s own regret).
  7. Honest but dull: measure the loop in playtests and cheat the slow part (Naughty Dog).
- For a deterministic sim there is one extra failure the shooters did not have: order dependence. Every referee decision (who gets the token, who steals it) needs a fixed tie break, such as lowest unit index, and requests must be resolved from an ordered list. Greedy assignment as in Halo 3 is fine if the scan order is fixed.
- The passive crowd complaint about kung fu circle games is the risk on the other side: a cap that is too tight makes four of five units look like extras. The weighted budget (several light actions plus one heavy) and visible waiting behaviours are the published answers.
- Tooling and readability for the owner matter as much as for Bungie's designers. Isla's goals of transparency and "being able to reason about the system" argue for a priority list a person can read top to bottom over a scorer whose output has to be debugged.

### Gaps
- No studio source on failures specific to auto battlers or to deterministic lockstep AI. Everything here is from real time action games, so the transfer points are inference.
- No primary source on FromSoftware, Arkham, Ghost of Tsushima or Total War failure modes and fixes.
- The God of War and Doom items rest on a machine made summary and a commentator's unnamed sources.
